3. Results

3.1 Comparative performance

The two architectures were trained under one protocol. Both receive the same ten channels, nine elevations of reflectivity and a validity mask, and both are optimised with the same focal-Tversky loss, the same patch sampler, and a 100-epoch schedule with patience 50. Seeds 42–46 change the initialisation and the sequence of random crops. The test split contains 161 positive scans and 147 negative scans, drawn from 21 migration nights and 19 quiet nights, and shares no night with training or validation. Every run selected the probability threshold 0.15 on validation. That cutoff was not touched again.

Table 1 and Figure 1 summarise the test set. Macro Dice, the unweighted mean of per-scene Dice on the positive scans, is 0.526 ± 0.005 for the Attention U-Net and 0.517 ± 0.009 for the U-Net. Pixel-pooled Dice on those scans is 0.654 ± 0.006 and 0.651 ± 0.003. The Attention U-Net recalls more of the plume (0.594 ± 0.033 against 0.569 ± 0.030) and scores higher on both boundary measures. The U-Net is the more precise of the two (0.553 ± 0.020 against 0.538 ± 0.021). None of these gaps is large beside the spread across seeds, and none is large beside the shift from validation to test reported below.

3.2 Ablation of the attention gates

The Attention U-Net is the U-Net with attention gates on the skip connections and nowhere else. The paired contrast is therefore an ablation of that module. It is evaluated on the same scans, at the same threshold, and the uncertainty is a night-clustered percentile interval (2,000 resamples of the 21 positive-test nights, seed 0). The pre-specified pair is seed 42.

On that pair the gates change Dice by +0.0037 (95% interval −0.0084 to +0.0170). IoU, precision and recall likewise have intervals that include zero. NSD changes by +0.0206 (0.0082 to 0.0319) and fuzzy boundary F1 by +0.0179 (0.0062 to 0.0290). Adding the gates does not produce a resolved gain in overlap. It does produce a resolved gain in the two boundary scores, for this one seed (Figure 2).

Repeating the ablation at the other four seeds shows that the overlap result is stable in sign and unstable in significance, and that the boundary result is not stable in sign (Figure 3, Table 2). Dice is positive at every seed. The interval excludes zero at three seeds and includes it at two, including the pre-specified seed. The mean of the five Dice differences is about +0.010. NSD is positive at four seeds and negative at seed 43, where the interval (−0.0133 to −0.0020) excludes zero in the plain U-Net’s favour. A module that helps the boundary at one seed and hurts it at another is not a module one would carry forward on the strength of a single paired test.

3.3 Robustness

Three checks ask whether the comparison depends on the seed, the year, or the tolerance at which a boundary is counted as correct. The threshold stays at 0.15 throughout.

Across years the two models move together (Figure 6). Both are weak in 2013 (14 positive test scans; mean Dice 0.37 and 0.33) and stronger in 2014 and 2019 (about 0.66 and 0.63, then 0.66 and 0.65). The Attention U-Net is ahead in six of the seven years. In 2018, on 16 scans, the U-Net is ahead by 0.002. The year-to-year range is nearly 0.3 Dice. The architecture gap inside any one year is a few hundredths at most. A model that has learned the plume does so in every year; the years themselves are not interchangeable.

The boundary score depends smoothly on tolerance, and the ordering at 2 px is not an artefact of that particular choice (Figure 5). For seed 42 the Attention U-Net lies above the U-Net from 1 px to 20 px. The night-clustered bands overlap, so the curves should be read as a small and persistent offset, not as a separation that is resolved at every distance. That reading agrees with Figure 3: the offset is real at some seeds and reversed at another.

Validation macro Dice lies between 0.553 and 0.571. Test macro Dice lies between 0.504 and 0.532. The mean drop is 0.041 for the Attention U-Net and 0.042 for the U-Net. The drop is a property of the split, not of the decoder. Selecting the architecture on validation would have preferred the Attention U-Net, and the test set agrees with the sign of that preference, but either validation number overstates the corresponding test number by about four points.

3.4 Detection, and the ceiling from a perfect presence gate

A second reading of the same masks treats the problem as detection rather than overlap. A scan score is the area of the mask at the locked threshold. A night score is either the maximum or the mean of the scan scores inside that operational night. Figure 6 shows the test ROC and the precision–recall curve for seed 42. Scan ROC area is 0.83 for the Attention U-Net and 0.84 for the U-Net. The night maximum gives 0.81 and 0.82. The night mean gives 0.86 and 0.86. Average precision ranks the three scores in the same order. The two decoders are not separable. The horizontal line on the precision–recall panel is the prevalence of positive scans in this cohort. Negatives were subsampled to match the positives, so that average precision is not the rate an operator would see in the raw archive.

An oracle that knows which scans are quiet, and blanks them, leaves every positive scan unchanged. Macro Dice therefore cannot move. The measured change is 0.0000 for both seed-42 models. Global Dice, which charges false pixels on quiet scans against the score, rises by 0.051 (0.6047 to 0.6562 for the Attention U-Net, and 0.5955 to 0.6467 for the U-Net). Knowing presence is worth about five points of integrated overlap and nothing on the per-plume Dice. The false-alarm rate at a minimum area of 25 km² remains 0.56 ± 0.05 and 0.55 ± 0.05: a little over half of the quiet test scans still contain a false region of that size, in both families. Sensitivity retained at the same area is 0.94 and 0.93.

4. Discussion

4.1 The decoder is not the dominant factor

Studies that compare a pointwise classifier with a spatial network on weather-radar echoes usually find that spatial context is the largest single gain, and that extra inputs help the weaker architecture more than the stronger one. Both models in the present comparison are already spatial networks. They see the same nine elevations and the same validity mask. The only designed difference is whether the skip connections are gated. Under that control, the gates move test Dice by about one point, a shift smaller than the seed range of the U-Net and an order of magnitude smaller than the range across years. The result is not that attention is useless. It is that, once a U-Net is given the full elevation volume, gating the skips is a second-order change. Carrying the heavier decoder forward would have to be justified by the boundary scores, and those scores do not keep a consistent sign.

4.2 Overlap and boundary answer different questions

Dice treats a plume as a set of pixels and is dominated by the interior of large plumes. NSD and fuzzy boundary F1 treat it as a contour. The Attention U-Net’s higher recall and lower precision are the signature of a contour drawn slightly wider, which can raise a boundary score even when the overlap score barely moves. That is what seed 42 shows, and what three of the five seeds show for NSD. Seed 43 shows the opposite contour error, with an interval that excludes zero. A paper that reported only seed 42 would have concluded that the gates improve the boundary. The five-seed check withdraws that conclusion. The honest statement is that the gates reshuffle the contour error from seed to seed, and that the reshuffling is large enough to change the sign.

4.3 Where the remaining error sits

The oracle makes the location of the error quantitative. Blanking every quiet scan, which is the best a presence classifier can do for the segmenter, does not improve the Dice of the plumes that are present. It improves only the score that penalises false pixels on quiet nights, and only by about 0.05. The per-plume Dice near 0.52 is therefore not a detection failure. It is a failure to draw the plume at the right width and in the right place, and it is shared by both decoders. The false-alarm rate near 0.56 at 25 km² says the same thing from the quiet nights: both models still paint a substantial false region on more than half of them. A presence model can hide those regions. It cannot repair the plume.

The night-mean score detects migration nights more reliably than the night-maximum score (ROC area 0.86 against 0.81–0.82). The maximum is set by a single scan and is sensitive to one false blob. The mean asks whether the elevated area persists through the night. For an archive in which a migration night contains many scans, the mean is the more stable reduction. Neither reduction distinguishes the two decoders.

4.4 What this experiment does not support

The five Swin-Tiny classifiers have validation ROC areas of 0.941, 0.948, 0.927, 0.919 and 0.932. The seed-42 weights required to score those probabilities on the test set are not available, so Figure 6 does not include a classifier curve, and no claim is made about test-set detection from the Swin model. No cascade was evaluated. No ablation of the input channels, the loss, or a temporal encoder was run on this frozen split; earlier screening runs used a different assignment of nights and are not comparable to Table 1. The historical model trained on a scan-level split, in which most nights appeared in more than one of train, validation and test, is omitted for the same reason. A Dice lower than that model’s published figure is the expected result of removing the leakage, not evidence that the retrain failed.

Night-level ROC areas rest on about forty nights. They are reported as a ranking of score definitions, not as a precise operational rate. The intervals in Figures 2 and 3 resample nights, not scans, because scans inside a night are not independent. They remain intervals for one radar and seven seasons.
